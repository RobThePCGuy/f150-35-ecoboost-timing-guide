# Stage 6: Support the axle; remove the oil pan and oil pump

The oil pump bolts to the bottom of the block, so the pan has to come down. On a 4WD truck that means removing the starter and a crossmember and lowering the front axle on a jack for clearance. With the pan off, you pull the crank sprocket, the pickup and the pump, then clean and inspect the pickup so it can go back on.

**2WD trucks:** this page follows Ford's 4X4 branch of Oil Pan – Removal. Open Ford's 4X2 branch and follow it in place of steps 11–15; check every other step against it too.

**Video:** [Oil pump video at 2:00](https://youtu.be/NJ2PYXTVjkI?t=120) — a different method: pickup and pump out with the oil pan left on. The pickup and pump removal parts (3:00 to 13:00) still show the bolts and how to break the pump free

**Have open:**
- Ford workshop manual, Section 303-01A: Oil Pan – Removal (front cover already removed, 4X4)
- Section 303-06: Starter Motor – Removal and Installation (steps 3–8)
- Section 303-01A: Oil Pump – Removal (steps 18–20)
- Section 501-02: Front End Body Panels – Exploded View, for the air dam

**Tools / parts / time limits:**
- Truck on rated stands, hydraulic jack and a ratchet strap for the axle
- 8, 10, 13 and 18 mm sockets; trim tool; flat pry tool
- A cardboard outline of the pan with all 16 bolt holes, numbered per Ford's pan bolt figure
- Plastic scraper, silicone gasket remover, metal surface prep; a 3M Roloc white bristle disc (07528) for the pan only
- Parts cleaner, compressed air, flashlight
- New oil filter and new pickup O-ring on hand; Melling 397S pickup if the old one fails inspection

## Steps

1. **Remove the splash shield** (4 fasteners). On many trucks these are plastic quarter-turn retainers, on some they're bolts. For quarter-turns, turn about 90° with a flat screwdriver and pull. Free the two at one end first, then hold the shield up while you free the other two. (Ford figure N0129112 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
   Bag B06-01: 4 quarter-turn fasteners / push pins, back in Stage 9
2. **Remove the skid plate if there is one** (4 bolts). No skid plate, skip this.
   Bag B06-02: 4 × 13 mm, back in Stage 9, 48 Nm
3. **Remove the front air dam.** Use the Section 501-02 exploded view for its fasteners.
   Bag B06-03: 2 bolts on Kansas City-built trucks, or 2 nuts with their 2 retainers on Dearborn-built trucks (socket size unconfirmed; check the 501-02 view), back in Stage 9
4. **Free the transmission cooler tubes from the pan area.** Take out the bracket bolt first. Then open the two plastic clips around the steel lines: lift the latch tab with a small screwdriver and swing the clip open. Pull on the clip, never bend the lines. (Ford figure N0147997 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
   Bag B06-04: 1 × 10 mm, back in Stage 9, 12 Nm
5. **Remove the oil filter** and throw it away.
6. **Remove the starter terminal cover.** The battery is already disconnected. Ease the cap off both solenoid terminals without tearing it.
   Bag B06-05: terminal cover, back in Stage 9
7. **Disconnect the big B+ cable** (nut on the large stud). Spin the nut back onto the stud or bag it labeled "B+ big nut."
   Bag B06-06: 1 × 13 mm, back in Stage 9, 12 Nm
8. **Disconnect the small S-terminal wire.** This stud is small and strips easily. Spin the nut back on or bag it apart from the B+ nut.
   Bag B06-07: 1 × 10 mm (some trucks 8 mm), back in Stage 9, 5 Nm
9. **Remove the second cooler tube bracket nut near the starter** and move the lines aside. This is a separate fastening from step 4. Remove it even though the lines already moved.
   Bag B06-08: 1 × 10 mm, back in Stage 9, 12 Nm
10. **Remove the starter.** Take out the lower stud bolt and move the ground cable eyelet aside, then take out the upper stud bolt and lift the starter out. Tape a "LOWER + GROUND" label on the lower one.
    Bag B06-09: 2 × 13 mm stud bolts, back in Stage 9, 48 Nm, upper first
11. **Remove the crossmember** (4 bolts and 4 nuts). (Ford figure N0085027 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
    Bag B06-10: 4 bolts + 4 nuts, 18 mm likely, back in Stage 9, 90 Nm
12. **Support the front axle.** Set a hydraulic jack under the front axle and strap the axle to it tight. The axle rides on that jack until Stage 9. (Ford figure GE0586B in "Oil Pan – Removal")
13. **Remove the upper axle carrier bushing bolt.** Label it "UPPER." (Ford figure A0045775 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
    Bag B06-11: 1 × 18 mm, back in Stage 9, 115 Nm
14. **Remove the axle tube carrier bushing bolt.** Label it "AXLE TUBE." (Ford figure GE0588A in "Oil Pan – Removal")
    Bag B06-12: 1 × 18 mm, back in Stage 9, 115 Nm
15. **Remove the lower axle carrier bushing bolt and lower the axle.** Label it "LOWER." Leave the front driveshaft and transmission in place; Ford's figure hides the driveshaft only so you can see the bolt. Lower the jack slowly, just far enough for the pan to clear, and watch the vacuum lines to the axle solenoid so they don't pull off or kink. (Ford figure A0045774 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
    Bag B06-13: 1 × 18 mm, back in Stage 9, 115 Nm
16. **Remove the pan-to-bellhousing bolt below the starter.** Bag it on its own; it may be a different length from the LH pair. (Ford figure N0113914 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
    Bag B06-14: 1 × 13 mm (possibly 15 mm), back in Stage 9, 48 Nm after pan stage 3
17. **Remove the 2 LH bellhousing-to-pan bolts.** (Ford figure N0113916 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
    Bag B06-15: 2 × 13 mm (possibly 15 mm), back in Stage 9, 48 Nm after pan stage 3
18. **Remove the 16 pan bolts.** Push each one through its numbered hole in the cardboard outline as it comes out. (Ford figure N0114753 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
    Bag B06-16: bolt 10 alone, 10 mm likely, back in Stage 9, 3 Nm, then 20 Nm + 90° last
    Bag B06-17: bolts 11, 13, 14, 10 mm likely, back in Stage 9, 3 Nm, then 20 Nm + 45°
    Bag B06-18: bolts 1–9 and 12, 10 mm likely, back in Stage 9, 20 Nm + 45°
    Bag B06-19: bolts 15 and 16, 8 mm, back in Stage 9, 10 Nm + 45°
19. **Pry the pan down.** Check every bolt is out. Put a flat pry tool in the cast pry pocket at the LH rear, twist to open the joint, then do the same at the RH front. Alternate until the RTV lets go. Never drive the blade into the seam anywhere else. Lower the pan level so the oil in it stays put. (Ford figure N0113919 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
20. **Unclip the generator harness retainer** from the block for clearance. Slide a forked trim tool under the clip base and pry straight out. (Ford figure N0121869 in "Oil Pan – Removal")
    Bag B06-20: 1 harness clip, back in Stage 9
21. **Clean the pan.** On the pan only, use the white 3M Roloc bristle disc (07528) at about 15,000 rpm. No metal scrapers, wire brushes or other abrasive discs. Wash the pan thoroughly afterward so no grit stays in it.
22. **Clean the block and front cover sealing faces by hand.** No bristle disc, wire brush or power abrasive here; the grit ends up in the engine. Scrape off the big chunks with plastic, soak with silicone gasket remover for several minutes, wipe it off, repeat if needed, then wipe with metal surface prep. Stained metal is fine; don't chase shiny.
23. **Remove the crank sprocket.** Slide it off the crank nose. Before it comes off, check the crank dot is still where it was in Stage 5. The most common timing mistake reported in the comments is the crank turning while the turning bolt comes out with the chain off; this is an interference engine, so if it moved, turn it back to the mark gently and never force it against a valve (common report in the video comments). This covers the sprocket removal in the timing procedure too; don't look for it twice. (Ford figure N0148008 in "Oil Pump – Removal")
24. **Remove the pickup and screen** (3 bolts). Throw away the O-ring. (Ford figure N0120779 in "Oil Pump – Removal")
    Bag B06-21: 3 × 8 mm, back in Stage 8, 10 Nm
    The oil pump video shows two 8 mm bolts at the pump end of the pickup. Ford counts three for the pickup, so find and bag all three before you pull it.
25. **Remove the oil pump** (3 bolts). (Ford figure N0148009 in "Oil Pump – Removal")
    Bag B06-22: 3 × 8 mm, back in Stage 8, 10 Nm
    Tip: the pump sticks to the block. Break the pickup O-ring loose first, then work the pump straight off evenly. Pry only on the plain cast area of the block. The machined face where the pump's oil outlet meets the block has no gasket; a pry mark there is a leak path, so pull that side by hand (from FordTechMakuloco's video).
26. **Inspect the sump.** Look at what's in the pan and on the screen. Plastic bits are worn guide material and are expected. Metal flakes are not: stop and find where they came from before you rebuild. Bag anything you find.
27. **Clean and check the original pickup for reuse.** Soak it in parts cleaner. Then spray cleaner and blow compressed air in through the tube end, so dirt goes back out the screen side instead of deeper in. Shine a light through the tube to confirm it's clear. Pass it only if the screen has no tears, holes, crushed spots or loose edges, the tube has no cracks, the bracket isn't bent, and the flange face is clean with no nicks. If any check fails, replace it with a Melling 397S. Let it dry completely. It goes back in Stage 8 with a new O-ring.

## Done when
- Starter out, crossmember out, axle lowered and strapped to the jack (4WD)
- Pan off, pan cleaned, block and cover faces cleaned by hand
- Crank sprocket, pickup and pump off
- Sump and screen inspected, debris bagged, no unexplained metal
- Pickup cleaned and passed, or a Melling 397S on hand
- Bags B06-01 through B06-22 filled and labeled; pan bolts in the cardboard outline

## Stop if
- The truck or axle support feels unstable, or there isn't clearance without forcing it
- An axle vacuum line pulls off or cracks
- You find metal debris, or guide pieces are missing and not in the pan
- The pump or crank shows signs of oil starvation
