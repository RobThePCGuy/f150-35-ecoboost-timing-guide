# Stage 5: Position the cams and remove the primary chain

With the front cover off, you turn the engine to its timing position, lock both banks' camshafts with the holding plates, record where the old chain sits, and take off the primary chain, its tensioner, its arm and the lower left guide. The crank sprocket stays on for now. It comes off in Stage 6, after the oil pan is down.

Keep the Ford procedure open beside this page for the diagrams and exact wording on every stage.

**Have open:** Ford workshop manual, Section 303-01A: Timing Drive Components – Removal (steps 2–15).

**Tools / parts / time limits:**
- Old crank bolt plus a short spacer (deep socket or short pipe) and an 18 mm socket with a breaker bar, to turn the crank with the pulley off
- Camshaft Holding Tool 303-1248, plates that fit both banks
- 8 mm socket, paint pen or white-out for marking links, labeled trays per bank
- Hand tools only on the timing parts; clean caps or bags for the oil control solenoids
- Settle how you will turn the crank before you start. If you don't have a sound method, stop here.

## Steps

1. **Turn the engine to the timing marks.** Thread the old crank bolt back into the crank with the spacer under its head, resting on the sprocket. Snug it by hand. Turn **clockwise only**, slowly, with the 18 mm socket and breaker bar. Stop when the painted marks on both intake phasers sit where Ford's photo shows them: near the top of each phaser, toward that bank's solenoids. Match the photo, don't guess by clock position. If it binds, stop and find out why. Never turn the engine by a cam bolt or against the holding plates. (Ford figure N0122132 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
   In the video the two intake phaser dots point up and outward at roughly 30° to each side, the only crank position where both do it at once, and the crank dot sits around 4 to 5 o'clock. If the dots aren't both there when the cam flats come level, turn the crank one more full turn; the cams run at half speed (from FordTechMakuloco's video).
2. **Remove the LH valve train oil tube** (3 bolts). (Ford figure N0118126 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
   Bag B05-01: 3 × 8 mm, back in Stage 10, 8 Nm + 45°
3. **Lock the LH cams.** Fit the 303-1248 plate onto the flats of both LH camshafts. It holds them at top dead center while the chain is off. It is a positioning tool, not a breaker-bar brace. (Ford figure N0118121 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
   The plate should drop onto the flats and touch the head on both sides without force. If it hangs up, nudge the crank clockwise a hair until it falls in (from FordTechMakuloco's video). Some aftermarket plate sets are machined too tight to drop on, and some are sloppy enough to let the cams move; if yours won't seat or wobbles on the flats, stop and get a better set before the chain comes off (common report in the video comments).
4. **Remove the RH valve train oil tube** (3 bolts). (Ford figure N0118127 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
   Bag B05-02: 3 × 8 mm, back in Stage 10, 8 Nm + 45°
5. **Lock the RH cams** with the other 303-1248 plate on the RH cam flats. (Ford figure N0118122 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
6. **Mark the chain if the old colored links are gone.** Skip this step if you can still see the factory colored links. Otherwise paint the link lined up with the LH intake phaser mark, then the link at the RH intake phaser mark, then the two links that straddle the crank sprocket dot (the dot sits between them). Photograph the whole front with marks visible before you go further. (Ford figures N0120879, N0120880 and N0148175 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
7. **Remove the primary tensioner** (2 bolts). (Ford figure N0143530 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
   Bag B05-03: 2 × 8 mm, back in Stage 10, 10 Nm
8. **Remove the tensioner arm.** The long plastic arm hangs on a pivot pin through the hole at its top. Pull it straight forward off the pin, wiggling if it sticks. Don't pry against the sprocket teeth. (Ford figure N0148158 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
9. **Remove the lower LH primary guide** (2 bolts). Keep these apart from the single upper LH guide bolt that comes out in Stage 7; the lengths may differ. (Ford figure N0148159 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
   Bag B05-04: 2 × 8 mm, back in Stage 10, 10 Nm
10. **Pull the LH intake oil control solenoid** (1 bolt). Twist it slightly as you pull. Cap or bag it right away so no dirt gets on it. Pulling the solenoids gives the chain room to come off. (Ford figure N0118136 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
    Bag B05-05: 1 × 8 mm, back in Stage 10, 8 Nm + 20°
11. **Pull the RH intake oil control solenoid** (1 bolt) the same way and keep it clean. (Ford figure N0118137 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
    Bag B05-06: 1 × 8 mm, back in Stage 10, 8 Nm + 20°
12. **Lift off the primary chain.** Lay it out next to the new one and keep it with the old tensioner and guides. Note any broken guide pieces or chewed plastic. (Ford figure N0148176 in "Timing Drive Components – Removal")
    Gather all the slack at the top and angle the chain past each phaser; it's a long, stiff chain and a two-handed job (from FordTechMakuloco's video).
13. **Stop here.** Leave the crank sprocket on. It comes off in Stage 6 after the oil pan is out.

## Done when
- Both intake phaser marks match Ford's photo, and both 303-1248 plates sit fully on the cam flats
- Old links marked (if needed) and photographed
- Primary chain, tensioner, arm and lower LH guide off; both intake solenoids out, clean and capped
- Crank sprocket still in place
- Bags B05-01 through B05-06 filled and labeled

## Stop if
- The marks and the holding plates disagree, or a plate won't seat
- A cam moves on its own once the chain is slack
- A holding plate lifts or slips. Several people report a plate letting go with the chain off: the cams spun, a valve stopped against a piston, and the crank wouldn't turn. Don't turn anything; get the cams back to their marks first (common report in the video comments)
- You find broken guide pieces you can't account for, or a damaged bore
- You have no sound way to turn the crank clockwise
