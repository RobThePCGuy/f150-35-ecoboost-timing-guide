# Stage 4: Drain and remove the front cover

Drain oil and coolant, strip the front of the engine (belts, thermostat housing, water pump, alternator, tensioner, crank pulley and seal), and pull the timing cover. Do every belt-related crank turn in this stage, before any cam plates go on in Stage 5.

**Have open:** Ford workshop manual, Section 303-01A: Engine Front Cover – Removal, Crankshaft Pulley – Removal, Crankshaft Front Seal – Removal; Section 303-03: Cooling System Draining, Thermostat Housing – Removal, Coolant Pump – Removal; Section 303-05: Accessory Drive Belt, A/C Compressor Belt – Removal (3.5L).

**Tools / parts / time limits:**
- Engine cold before you open the cooling system.
- Drain pans, waterproof plastic to cover the belts, hose-clamp pliers.
- 15 mm socket and long bar (tensioner), 18 mm socket and breaker bar (crank), strap wrench, 3-jaw puller, crank seal remover, flat pry bar, stretch-belt tool (Ford 303-1419 or equivalent).
- Discard: crank pulley bolt, crank front seal, water pump gasket and O-ring, thermostat housing gasket and O-ring.
- Use the 3.5L A/C belt branch. The steps that cut the belt are for the 5.0L only.

## Steps
1. **Drain the oil.** Pull the drain plug, let it empty, put the plug back.
   Bag B04-01: 1 × 15 mm, reinstalled now, 27 Nm (20 lb-ft).
2. **Crack the 4 water pump pulley bolts loose** while the belt is still on; its tension holds the pulley still. Don't remove them yet.
3. **Remove the accessory belt.** Photograph the routing. 15 mm socket on a long bar on the tensioner hex, pull counterclockwise. The arm swings and the belt goes slack. If the arm doesn't move and the bolt itself starts to turn, stop and check the tensioner against Ford's accessory drive figure before forcing it. Slip the belt off the smooth idler and ease the tensioner back.
4. **Cover the A/C belt** completely with waterproof plastic so coolant can't reach it.
5. **Drain the coolant.** With the engine cold, wrap a thick rag around the degas bottle cap and turn it slowly counterclockwise until pressure starts to hiss. Step back until it stops, then remove the cap. Open the radiator draincock over a pan, drain, and close it.
   Tip: the draincock is the red knob on the passenger side lower radiator. Push a 5/16 in hose onto its nipple and run it into the pan, or the coolant splashes all over the frame rail. It drains much faster once the degas cap is off (from FordTechMakuloco's video).
6. **Remove the LH air cleaner-to-turbo pipe and LH turbo-to-CAC pipe** if not already out from Stage 3. Cover all accessory belts with plastic.
7. **Oil cap:** if it's off, put it back on so nothing falls in.
8. **Unplug the block heater** if you have one (cord tucked behind the grille). Grip the plug body, not the cord.
9. **Remove the bolt and the LH turbo-to-CAC tube bracket** at the thermostat housing. (Ford figure in "Thermostat Housing – Removal", step 6.)
   Bag B04-02: 1 × 8 mm, back in Stage 12, 6 Nm (53 lb-in).
10. **Disconnect the 4 hoses from the thermostat housing:** degas bottle hose, lower radiator hose, heater outlet hose, upper radiator hose. Each is a spring clamp: squeeze with hose-clamp pliers, slide it back past the bead, twist the hose and pull straight off. Never pry under the hose against the neck.
    The lower hose and the crossover hold a lot of coolant; have a container under each one. The cavity behind the thermostat housing stays full, so suck it out with a vacuum bleeder or fluid extractor before you lift the housing (from FordTechMakuloco's video).
11. **Remove the 4 thermostat housing bolts.**
    Bag B04-03: 4 × 8 mm, back in Stage 12, 8 Nm + 45° in Ford's sequence.
12. **Lift off the thermostat housing.** Hold the coolant inlet pipe in place by the ear on its top so it doesn't pull out of the crossover; if it does, its O-ring can be damaged. Throw away the gasket and O-ring.
13. **Remove the 4 bolts and the water pump pulley.**
    Bag B04-04: 4 × 10 mm, back in Stage 12, 25 Nm (18 lb-ft).
14. **Remove the 7 bolts and the water pump.** Throw away its gasket and O-ring.
    Before you loosen these, suck the coolant out of the cavity behind the pump with an extractor hose; the video pulls out about three containers' worth. Lay a rag below the pump so coolant doesn't run down into the crankcase (from FordTechMakuloco's video).
    Bag B04-05: 7 × 8 mm, back in Stage 12, 10 Nm + 45° in Ford's sequence.
15. **Remove the alternator bolt** and swing the alternator aside.
    Tip: the lower ear of the alternator has an adjuster sleeve that gets pushed in when the bolt is tightened. Tap it back out once the alternator is off so it can reset at reassembly (from FordTechMakuloco's video).
    Bag B04-06: 1 bolt (15 mm), back in Stage 12, 48 Nm (35 lb-ft).
16. **Remove the alternator stud** (double-nut it with two M10 nuts, or use its own drive if it has one).
    Bag B04-07: 1 stud, back in Stage 12, 8 Nm (71 lb-in).
    The video shows a 7 mm hex on the end of the stud, so try that before double-nutting it.
17. **Remove the 2 bolts and the accessory belt tensioner.**
    Bag B04-08: 2 × 10 mm, back in Stage 12, 25 Nm (18 lb-ft).
18. **Remove the A/C stretch belt.** Set the stretch-belt tool on the belt at the compressor pulley exactly where Ford figure N0115832 in "A/C Compressor Belt – Removal" shows it. Turn the crank **clockwise** with the 18 mm socket on the crank bolt, slowly, by hand, no impact. Let the tool feed in evenly around about half the pulley (Ford figure N0115833). Then fold the tool around the inside of the belt and pull it firmly from below in one quick motion; the belt jumps off. If the crank won't turn smoothly, stop. Never turn it counterclockwise.
19. **Remove the crank pulley bolt.** Hold the pulley with the strap wrench and break the bolt loose with the 18 mm. Throw the bolt away; keep the washer.
    Bag B04-09: 1 × 18 mm, NEW at Stage 12, 120 Nm, loosen one full turn, 50 Nm, then +90°.
    Bag B04-10: washer, back in Stage 12.
20. **Pull the crank pulley** with the 3-jaw puller.
    The pulley has no threaded puller holes. Hook the three jaws behind the three spokes and press against the crank nose with the puller's center pin (from FordTechMakuloco's video).
21. **Remove the crank front seal** with a seal remover and throw it away. Clean the seal bore with metal surface prep.
22. **Pop the push-pin retainer** off the oil pan (behind the trans cooler lines). Reach between the lines; don't bend them.
    Bag B04-11: push pin, back in Stage 11.
23. **Remove the nut and the trans cooler tube bracket** from the front cover stud.
    Bag B04-12: 1 × 10 mm, back in Stage 11, 12 Nm (106 lb-in).
24. **Free the alternator harness** clip from the front cover stud with a forked trim tool and move the harness away.
25. **Remove the 22 front cover bolts.** (Ford figure N0120120 in "Engine Front Cover – Removal" shows all locations.) Bag them by the groups below; positions matter at install. Sockets: 13 mm (field-confirmed: every front cover bolt is 13 mm except one 15 mm at the lower right and one small 8 mm at the top left, as you face the engine). Push each one through the cardboard template in its hole so the odd sizes go back where they came from.
    Bag B04-13: 9 × 13 mm (positions 1, 2, 7, 8, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20), back in Stage 11, 10 Nm all in sequence, then 20 Nm + 45° in sequence.
    Bag B04-14: 1 larger bolt, position 21 (13 mm), back in Stage 11, 10 Nm with the first group, then 20 Nm + 90°.
    Bag B04-15: 11 × 13 mm (positions 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16), back in Stage 11, 10 Nm then 20 Nm + 45°.
    Bag B04-16: 1 small bolt (8 mm), position 22, back in Stage 11, 10 Nm + 45°.
   The video calls these bolts 10 mm; on the truck this guide came from they measured 13 mm, so start with 13 mm.
26. **Pry the front cover off at the 7 pry pads only.** (Ford figure N0120123.) Facing the engine: each top outer corner, the inner top of the RH (passenger) head lobe, three on the LH (driver) lower edge, and the bottom RH (passenger) corner. Check the figure for your exact spots. Flat bar on the cast tab, never in the seam. Work tab to tab until the sealant lets go, then pull the cover straight forward.
27. **Inspect the radial seal** on the back of the cover. It seals around the coolant tube that comes out through the front of the block (Ford calls it the water tube seal). Replace it only if damaged, pushing it out from the rear with Ford tool 205-150 or an equivalent driver. If the cover won't slide off, the seal is gripping that tube; pull the cover straight forward, slowly. If the old seal stays behind on the tube, work it off the tube now (from FordTechMakuloco's video).

## Done when
- Oil and coolant drained; drain plug back in at 27 Nm.
- Thermostat housing, water pump, alternator stud, tensioner, both belts, crank pulley and front seal off.
- Front cover off; all 22 bolts bagged by group.
- No cam plates installed yet.

## Stop if
- The crank won't turn by hand, or turns with a bind or tight spot.
- The seal bore or a pry pad is damaged.
- You find metal, sludge or coolant in the oil.
- Any component is hanging unsupported by a hose or harness.
