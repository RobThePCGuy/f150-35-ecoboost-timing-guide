# Stage 2: Relieve fuel pressure

Bleed off both fuel systems and cut battery power. This engine has a low-pressure side fed by the tank pump and a high-pressure side (direct injection) fed by the cam-driven pump on the LH valve cover. The high side can hold pressure long after shutdown, so you open it on purpose under a towel before touching any fuel line.

**Have open:** Ford workshop manual, Section 310-00: Fuel System Pressure Release; Section 414-01: Battery Disconnect and Connect.

**Tools / parts / time limits:**
- Safe way to get under the rear of the truck (jack stands or a lift), wheels chocked, transmission in Neutral only while it's properly supported.
- Shop towels, clean caps for opened fuel fittings, 8 mm socket, 17 mm flare-nut wrench.
- New high-pressure tube bracket nut on hand for Stage 14.
- No sparks or flame near the work. Fuel will seep when you crack the flare nut.

## Steps
1. **Support the truck.** Get it in the air on proper support with the transmission in Neutral.
2. **Unplug the fuel pump control module (FPCM).** It sits on top of the frame rail above the spare tire. If the connector has a colored lock, pull it back toward the wires first. Press the release tab and pull straight off; never pull on the wires. (Ford figure N0121097 in "Fuel System Pressure Release".)
3. **Run it dry.** Start the engine and let it idle until it stalls on its own.
4. **Crank it 5 more seconds** to pull down whatever is left in the rail.
5. **Key off.**
6. **Disconnect the battery ground only.** Loosen the clamp nut on the negative (black) post, twist the clamp off, and tuck it or wrap it in a rag so it can't fall back onto the post. The positive cable stays on. Leave the ground off until Stage 15.
   Bag B02-03: 1 × 8 mm clamp nut (stays on clamp), back in Stage 15, 5 Nm (44 lb-in).
7. **Remove the high-pressure tube bracket nut** near the pump on the LH valve cover and throw it away. This is the only time it comes off; Stage 3 won't remove it again.
   Bag B02-01: 1 × 8 mm nut, NEW at Stage 14, 8 Nm (71 lb-in) after the flare nut is fully tight.
8. **Open the high-pressure flare nut at the pump.** Wrap a shop towel around the nut where the steel tube enters the pump. With a 17 mm flare-nut wrench, turn it counterclockwise a little at a time until fuel seeps into the towel, then back it off and lift the tube clear. If the pump's own outlet fitting starts turning with the nut, stop and hold it with a backup wrench; never loosen a fitting on the pump body. Cap the tube end and the pump port.
   Bag B02-02: flare nut stays on the tube (reused), back in Stage 14, 15 Nm (133 lb-in) then +30°, or 50 Nm (37 lb-ft), whichever comes first. Oil the threads.
9. **Leave it open.** Don't reconnect, key on, or pressurize anything. The pump comes off in Stage 3 and the fuel system is closed up and tested in Stages 14–15.

## Done when
- Engine stalled on its own and was cranked 5 seconds after.
- FPCM unplugged; battery ground off and secured.
- Bracket nut discarded; flare nut open; both ends capped.

## Stop if
- The engine can't be safely run to stall (won't start, leaks, unsafe position). Get Ford's alternate relief method; don't improvise one.
- Fuel sprays instead of seeping when the flare nut cracks.
- A connector or fitting is damaged.
