# Bolts: what goes where

Every group of fasteners that comes off gets a bag number. Each stage page shows the bag on the step that takes it off and again on the step that puts it back.

## The system
1. Print the labels at the bottom and cut them apart. Get zip-top bags, a marker, painter's tape and two pieces of cardboard about the size of the front cover and a valve cover.
2. Set up two folding tables. **Table 1** is the top end (Stages 2–4), **Table 2** is timing and the bottom end (Stages 5–7). Tape a strip down each table and write the spot numbers on it before anything comes off.
3. As each step comes off, bag the fasteners with their label and set the bag on its spot. Big parts sit right after their bolts.
4. Where position matters (front cover, valve covers, oil pan), push each bolt through the cardboard in the same pattern as Ford's bolt map and number it.
5. Old timing parts and the old oil pump go straight into a box marked **OLD** under the table.
6. Reassembly starts at the far end of Table 2 and works back. Tick each bag as it empties. At the end every spot is empty; anything left is a missed step.

"Stays on" means loosen it and leave it on its part. "Trash" means Ford calls for new; don't bag the old ones. Front cover sockets, from the truck this guide came from: 13 mm (field-confirmed: every front cover bolt is 13 mm except one 15 mm at the lower right and one small 8 or 10 mm at the top left, as you face the engine). Match the 15 mm and the small bolt to their holes on your cardboard template. Socket sizes marked unconfirmed weren't confirmed for this engine; try the size listed and go one up or down if it's loose.
